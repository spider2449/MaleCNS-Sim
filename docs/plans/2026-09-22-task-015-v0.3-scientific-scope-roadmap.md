## 2. Current scientific evidence

The following statements are the evidence boundary inherited from the
committed checkpoint and Tasks 008-011. They are not expanded by Task 015.

### 2.1 Structural asymmetry and sugar connectivity

- `MN9_L` is MaleCNS body `10331`; `MN9_R` is body `16949` under the
  side-resolved correspondence used by the project.
- Full-graph total incoming anatomical weight is `6,012` for `MN9_L` and
  `556` for `MN9_R`, approximately a `10.8x` difference. The corresponding
  presynaptic-neuron counts are `278` and `137`.
- All four direct sugar-to-MN9 routes are empty: left sugar to either MN9 and
  right sugar to either MN9 have zero direct edges and zero anatomical weight.
- Two-hop and signed structural summaries differ substantially by route, but
  they are structural diagnostics. They are not firing predictors or causal
  explanations.

### 2.2 Dynamics and perturbation

- Simple short-path and signed structural proxies do not reliably predict MN9
  firing or perturbational influence in the validated model.
- The Task 009 candidate set was frozen before Task 010 perturbation results:
  `10135, 10313, 12752, 43765, 512730, 514753, 517861`.
- The frozen candidates produced heterogeneous effects, including increases,
  decreases, and exact nulls across candidate and stimulus-side conditions.
- Silencing `10313` increased `MN9_L` firing under the right-sugar condition
  by the recorded model-level effect, and silencing `10135` increased `MN9_L`
  firing under the left-sugar condition. These are model-sensitivity results.
- Structurally prominent candidates did not necessarily have strong model
  causal effects. In particular, `512730` had the strongest Task 009 signed
  structural proxy but weak or negligible MN9 effects in Task 010.

### 2.3 Task 011 classifications

Task 011 reused only five frozen cases, `10313`, `10135`, `12752`, `512730`,
and `43765`, at fixed trial indices `0`, `10`, and `20` for both 100 Hz
stimulus sides. Its accepted classifications are:

| Candidate | LEFT | RIGHT |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| `10313` | `NETWORK_REDISTRIBUTION_COMPATIBLE` | `NETWORK_REDISTRIBUTION_COMPATIBLE` |
| `10135` | `NETWORK_REDISTRIBUTION_COMPATIBLE` | `MIXED` |
| `12752` | `MIXED` | `MIXED` |
| `512730` | `NETWORK_REDISTRIBUTION_COMPATIBLE` | `UNRESOLVED` |
| `43765` | `UNRESOLVED` | `UNRESOLVED` |

The audit found early divergence followed by broad recurrent/network
divergence in active cases, while preserving mixed and unresolved cases where
the evidence did not support a single classification. These labels are
model-level compatibility classifications, not biological mechanisms.

## 3. Unresolved questions

The evidence leaves four materially different questions:

1. Are the Task 010/011 model-level effects and classifications robust to
   independently justified alternative neuron and synapse assumptions?
2. What anatomical organization accounts for the approximately `10.8x`
   difference in MN9 incoming weight?
3. Can the broad divergence in Task 011 be represented by reproducible network
   motifs rather than thousands of individual divergent neurons?
4. Which conclusions, if any, can be compared with independent published
   anatomical, electrophysiological, or behavioral observations?

These questions must remain separate. A structural answer is not a causal
answer, a model-robustness result is not biological validation, and agreement
with an external observation is not automatically mechanistic confirmation.

## 4. Candidate v0.3 research directions

### A. Robustness of mechanism classifications

#### Exact research question

Do the Task 010 perturbational findings and Task 011 compatibility
classifications survive independently justified changes in neuron and synapse
model assumptions, while the graph, identities, candidates, stimulus schedules,
and classification rules remain frozen?

#### Evidence motivating the question

Task 010 and Task 011 were validated under one reference LIF formulation. The
observed effects are heterogeneous and some classifications are mixed or
unresolved. Before treating network redistribution as a foundation for deeper
mechanistic analysis, its dependence on the reference formulation should be
measured explicitly.

#### Hypotheses

- H1, robustness: the predeclared Task 011 classification for most or all
  frozen case/side conditions is retained across a predeclared family of
  independently justified model variants.
- H2, model dependence: one or more predeclared variants systematically change
  classifications, effect direction, or propagation ordering.
- H3, ambiguity-localized: changes concentrate in the already mixed or
  unresolved cases rather than overturning the clearly classified cases.

These hypotheses are alternatives for a future preregistration; Task 015 does
not assign probabilities or select a favorable variant.

#### Falsifiable outcome

The primary outcome should be the predeclared retention or transition of the
ten Task 011 case/side classifications. Secondary outcomes may include signed
MN9 rate change, first-divergence ordering, propagation-window summaries, and
the Task 010 effect categories. A future preregistration must define the
retention threshold, variant family, and treatment of unresolved outcomes
before any variant result is inspected. Systematic reclassification would
falsify a strong robustness claim.

#### Required data and future model changes

- The v0.2.0 model source and the accepted Task 010/011 artifacts, digests,
  frozen candidate manifest, graph fingerprint, readout identities, schedules,
  and trial indices.
- An independently justified, predeclared variant matrix covering only the
  admissible dimensions. Candidate dimensions include membrane/synaptic
  dynamics, biologically justified parameter uncertainty, transmission-delay
  assumptions, unresolved-sign handling, and deterministic versus stochastic
  formulations.
- Any implementation changes must be isolated model variants or adapters. No
  variant may silently change the graph, identity mapping, sugar populations,
  frozen cases, or candidate-selection rule.
- Sign-policy variants require special treatment: resolved signs cannot be
  changed merely to fit an expected outcome, and unresolved signs must be
  represented by predeclared alternatives or excluded conditions.

No such model change is authorized or performed by Task 015.

#### Scientific suitability

New biological assumptions are needed only when a variant is supported by an
independent source or an explicit technical uncertainty. Fitting parameters or
choosing variants after seeing MN9 results is inadmissible. Post-hoc risk is
low to moderate if the variant matrix and primary outcome are frozen first;
otherwise it is high.

The computational cost is high because each frozen condition may require
multiple graph-scale replays. CPU correctness validation is possible and
required as the reference path. GPU acceleration remains valid for deterministic
float64 variants only after exact or otherwise predeclared CPU/GPU validation;
stochastic variants require a separate statistical equivalence gate and do not
inherit GPU validity automatically.

Expected scientific value is high: the study would distinguish a robust
model-level classification from a formulation-dependent observation without
claiming biological mechanism. Nonclaims include biological causality,
necessity, receptor physiology, and external validity.

Stopping criteria are: failed source or digest integrity; any unregistered
change to identities, candidates, populations, schedules, or classification
rules; a variant selected from observed results; CPU correctness failure;
unresolved CPU/GPU disagreement; or inability to justify the variant family
independently.

### B. Origin of MN9_L anatomical asymmetry

#### Exact research question

What anatomical organization accounts for the approximately `10.8x` difference
in total incoming anatomical weight between `MN9_L` and `MN9_R`?

#### Evidence motivating the question

Task 009 found `6,012` versus `556` total incoming anatomical weight, different
presynaptic-neuron counts, and different resolved, excitatory, inhibitory, and
unresolved components. Direct sugar-to-MN9 connectivity is absent, so the
asymmetry cannot be attributed to a direct sugar edge in the analyzed graph.

#### Hypotheses and falsifiable outcomes

Predeclared structural hypotheses could compare presynaptic population
composition, transmitter/sign composition, neuropil distribution, laterality,
high-weight contributor concentration, and pathway-depth distribution. Each
must be tested as a descriptive decomposition with fixed metrics. The outcome
would be a reproducible decomposition that either explains most of the weight
difference under a declared structural metric or leaves the asymmetry
unexplained. It would not identify a causal driver of firing.

#### Requirements and suitability

The study requires the full curated graph, annotations, resolved-sign metadata,
neuropil fields where available, and frozen MN9 identities. It need not change
the LIF model or run perturbations. New biological assumptions are not needed
for the first structural pass, but annotation semantics and missingness must be
declared. Post-hoc risk is moderate because many decompositions and cutoffs can
be chosen after inspection; a preregistered metric family and multiplicity
policy are required.

Computational cost is low to moderate relative to graph-scale dynamics. CPU
correctness validation is straightforward. GPU acceleration is unnecessary for
the descriptive analysis and would add no scientific authority. Expected value
is high for anatomy and provenance, but it does not directly test whether the
Task 011 model classifications are robust. It should be a later structural
study unless the robustness study is not preregisterable.

Stopping criteria are missing or unstable annotation fields, a decomposition
that depends on unregistered thresholds, or any attempt to convert structural
association into causal or dynamical explanation.

### C. Network redistribution motifs

#### Exact research question

Can the broad divergence observed in Task 011 be summarized into reproducible
network motifs rather than thousands of individual divergent neurons?

#### Evidence motivating the question

Task 011 observed early local divergence followed by broad expansion in active
cases, with classifications frequently compatible with network redistribution.
The current evidence does not establish which recurrent, convergent, divergent,
sign-balanced, feedforward, or temporal-community structures are responsible.

#### Hypotheses and falsifiable outcomes

Candidate hypotheses include recurrent-loop enrichment, convergence/divergence
hubs, sign-balanced motifs, feedforward versus recurrent propagation, and
stable temporal divergence communities. A falsifiable study would predefine the
motif representation, null graph or trace model, enrichment statistic, and
replication criterion, then test whether motifs reproduce across the frozen
cases and stimulus sides. Failure to reproduce across cases would reject a
single common motif summary.

#### Requirements and suitability

The study requires Task 011 sparse traces, the frozen graph and signs, and
fixed definitions of temporal windows and nulls. It may need analysis-only
code, but it must not discover new perturbation candidates or select cases from
motif results. New biological assumptions are not required for a model-level
motif analysis, although motif labels must not be presented as biological
circuits.

Post-hoc risk is moderate to high because motif vocabularies, community
thresholds, and null models can be adapted to observed traces. Computational
cost ranges from moderate to high, especially for graph-wide motif enumeration
and repeated nulls. CPU correctness validation is possible. GPU acceleration
would remain an implementation optimization only and would require separate
validation if traces are regenerated.

Expected value is medium to high, but the question is not cleanly
preregisterable until the motif ontology and null model are fixed independently
of Task 011 results. It is deferred for that reason.

Stopping criteria are undefined motif boundaries, candidate fishing, null-model
selection after seeing results, or a motif claim that cannot be separated from
the existing model-level compatibility labels.

### D. Cross-model / external biological validation

#### Exact research question

Which current MaleCNS-Sim conclusions can be compared with independent
published data or experimental observations, separately for anatomy,
electrophysiology, and behavior?

#### Evidence motivating the question

The checkpoint explicitly states that current results are model-level and that
no biological validation has been established. The absence of direct sugar-MN9
edges, MN9 anatomical asymmetry, model firing responses, and model-level
network classifications are possible comparison targets, not validated
matches.

#### Hypotheses and falsifiable outcomes

The three validation classes must remain separate:

- Anatomical: an independently defined mapping and metric may agree or
  disagree with the observed connectivity and asymmetry.
- Electrophysiological: independently measured response features may agree or
  disagree with the model under a declared stimulus mapping.
- Behavioral: an independently measured behavior may agree or disagree with a
  model-derived prediction only if the model contains the relevant behavioral
  variables.

The falsifiable outcome is agreement, disagreement, or insufficient comparable
measurement under criteria fixed before comparison. No result may be called
validation merely because it is qualitatively similar.

#### Requirements and suitability

This direction requires selected external datasets or publications, licensing
and provenance records, explicit anatomical and stimulus mappings, and a
predeclared comparison metric. It may require adapters or a reduced comparison
model, but it does not authorize changing the reference model to match an
external result. New biological assumptions are unavoidable for cross-dataset
mapping and must be separately justified.

Post-hoc risk is high because literature selection, endpoint selection, and
mapping choices can favor apparent agreement. Computational cost is uncertain
and may be high; CPU correctness applies to any software comparison but cannot
validate the external measurements. GPU acceleration is not intrinsically
required and would not turn an external comparison into biological validation.

Expected value is potentially high, but data availability and mapping validity
are not established in the current repository. The direction is deferred until
an independent dataset and comparison protocol are identified before results
are seen.

Stopping criteria are no independent dataset, non-comparable endpoints, unclear
provenance, post-hoc literature selection, or an attempted behavioral claim
without a behavioral model.

## 5. Evaluation criteria

Each candidate direction is judged against the same gates:

1. It must follow from a recorded v0.2.0 observation.
2. Its primary question and outcome must be falsifiable.
3. Its data, identities, candidates, and analysis population must be frozen
   before results are inspected.
4. It must not tune assumptions toward expected biology or discover candidates
   from the outcome being explained.
5. It must preserve the boundary between anatomy, model dynamics, and biology.
6. It must have a bounded computational protocol and a CPU correctness oracle.
7. Any GPU path must be validated as an implementation of the declared model,
   not treated as independent biological evidence.
8. It must define stopping criteria before execution.

## 7. Selected primary direction

The selected primary v0.3 direction is:

> Robustness of Task 010/011 model-level mechanism classifications under
> independently justified neuron and synapse model assumptions.

This is a scope decision only. It authorizes neither implementation nor
execution. The future study must begin with a preregistration and must use the
v0.2.0 model source, the accepted Task 011 digest, and the frozen Task 010/011
provenance as its baseline.

## 8. Scientific rationale

The current strongest conclusion is conditional: within one validated LIF
model, structural prominence and short-path structure do not consistently
predict perturbational influence, and several counterintuitive effects are
compatible with recurrent/network redistribution. That conclusion is useful
only if it is not an artifact of one unchallenged formulation.

Robustness is therefore the cleanest next question. It does not require new
candidate fishing, does not presume that a biological mechanism has already
been identified, and can be tested against the same frozen observations. It
also makes failure informative: a classification that changes under an
independently justified variant becomes a model-dependence result rather than
an invitation to select a preferred outcome.

## 9. Proposed hypothesis class

The future study should use a preregistered hypothesis class rather than a
single expected result:

- **Stable classification:** the accepted Task 011 labels are retained across
  the declared variant family.
- **Localized sensitivity:** changes occur mainly in `MIXED` or `UNRESOLVED`
  cases, while clear classifications remain stable.
- **Systematic model dependence:** one or more declared assumptions cause
  reproducible classification transitions or effect-direction changes.
- **Insufficient evidence:** a variant fails correctness, provenance, or
  comparability gates and is excluded without scientific interpretation.

The primary analysis must report the complete predeclared condition matrix,
including failures and unresolved outcomes. It must not report only variants
that support stability.

## 10. Required future preregistration

Before any Task 016 implementation or run, the preregistration must freeze:

- source commit/tag, graph and artifact fingerprints, Task 011 digest, MN9
  identities, sugar populations, frozen candidate cases, stimulus schedules,
  trial indices, seeds, and readouts;
- the model-variant dimensions and the independent justification for every
  variant;
- the handling of unresolved signs, stochasticity, delays, and parameter
  uncertainty;
- the primary classification-retention estimand and its threshold;
- secondary rate, timing, propagation, and network-wide metrics;
- CPU reference rules, deterministic replay rules, and CPU/GPU equivalence
  criteria for each variant;
- missing-data, failed-run, unresolved, and multiple-comparison rules;
- the complete stopping and exclusion criteria.

The preregistration must explicitly prohibit candidate discovery, identity
remapping, stimulus redesign, parameter fitting to MN9 or Task 011 outcomes,
retrospective threshold changes, and biological interpretation of model labels.

## 11. Required validation gates

The future study must pass all of these gates before interpretation:

1. Verify that the v0.2.0 source and all frozen Task 010/011 digests match the
   preregistration.
2. Replay the reference condition and require exact agreement with the
   accepted baseline summaries and Task 011 digest.
3. Verify that identities, candidate sets, sugar populations, graph
   fingerprints, sign policy, schedules, and readouts are unchanged except for
   explicitly registered model assumptions.
4. Validate every variant on CPU before using GPU acceleration.
5. For deterministic variants, require the declared CPU/GPU canonical-event
   and trace-equivalence gate. For stochastic variants, require a separately
   preregistered statistical equivalence gate; GPU results are not accepted by
   default.
6. Repeat a predeclared subset or all conditions and compare result digests or
   statistical replay summaries as appropriate.
7. Record every failed, excluded, unresolved, and completed condition without
   silently replacing it.
8. Review the complete diff and derived-artifact namespace before any commit or
   publication.

## 12. Non-goals

Task 015 does not:

- vary any parameter or model assumption;
- run a new experiment, perturbation, replay, or external validation;
- add, remove, or rediscover neuron identities, sugar populations, candidates,
  or readouts;
- change the Task 004 sign policy, Task 005 LIF parameters, graph, schedules,
  or Task 011 classification rules;
- claim a biological mechanism, biological causality, or external validation;
- create a new result artifact, change an existing result artifact, move or
  recreate `v0.2.0`, create a tag, or create a release;
- start Task 016 automatically.

## 13. Scientific nonclaims

The following remain in force:

- A connectome is not an executable biological brain.
- Model-level causal effects are not biological causality or biological
  necessity.
- `NETWORK_REDISTRIBUTION_COMPATIBLE` is a compatibility label within the
  declared model, not proof of a biological mechanism.
- MN9 side-resolved correspondence is not segmentation identity, lineage
  identity, or identical bilateral physiology.
- Anatomical weight, sign, effective model weight, activity, and perturbational
  influence are distinct quantities.
- Robustness across model variants, if later demonstrated, would establish
  model robustness only; it would not establish receptor physiology, an
  actual-fly mechanism, or behavioral necessity.
- CPU/GPU equivalence validates implementation consistency, not biological
  validity.
- No anatomical, electrophysiological, or behavioral external validation is
  established by the current repository.

## 14. Stopping conditions for v0.3 expansion

Scientific expansion must stop and be re-scoped if:

- the v0.2.0 source, accepted digest, or frozen provenance cannot be restored;
- a proposed variant requires unregistered identity, candidate, population,
  sign, schedule, or readout changes;
- the only support for a variant is an expected biological result or a fit to
  Task 010/011 outcomes;
- the primary outcome or classification rule is changed after results are
  visible;
- CPU correctness, deterministic replay, or the applicable CPU/GPU gate fails;
- a stochastic formulation cannot be compared under the preregistered rule;
- the study cannot distinguish model-level robustness from biological claims;
- the available compute or external data cannot satisfy the preregistered
  completeness and provenance requirements.

A failed gate is a result or a stop condition, not a reason to alter the frozen
question retrospectively.

## 15. Whether Task 016 is justified

Task 016 is scientifically justified only as a separate preregistration and
execution-gate task for Direction A. Its deliverable should be a complete
robustness protocol with the frozen variant matrix, primary estimand,
validation gates, exclusion rules, and stopping criteria. Task 016 is not yet
authorized to implement or execute the robustness study by this roadmap.

Directions B-D are deferred and must not be folded into Task 016. A later task
may revisit them only with its own question, preregistration, and evidence
boundary. Task 016 must not start automatically.
